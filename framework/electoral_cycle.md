# The electoral cycle as organizing spine

Source: Norris and the Electoral Integrity Project (Perceptions of
Electoral Integrity / PEI), which decomposes any national election
into **eleven sequential stages**, each measurable as an integrity
sub-dimension. This sequencing is the *temporal* spine of our
framework: any statute, in any country, allocates rules across these
stages, and any model statute we draft must do the same.

The PEI 11-stage cycle, with scope notes specific to our project (list
PR / MMP, local emphasis, non-partisan list administration):

| # | Stage | Project-specific scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Electoral laws** | The basic statutory architecture: which laws govern, at which level (national/sub-national), how amendable, and how stable. The Venice Commission's "regulatory levels and stability" principle lives here. |
| 2 | **Electoral procedures** | The administrative regulations that operationalize the laws: timelines, forms, certifications, ballot logistics. Often where list-administration mechanics are spelled out in detail. |
| 3 | **District boundaries** | District magnitude, who draws boundaries and how often, and the threshold-by-magnitude trade-off that is structurally critical to list PR. |
| 4 | **Voter registration** | Eligibility rules, registration mechanics, accuracy and completeness of the register. Upstream of list dynamics but determines the denominator for any threshold. |
| 5 | **Party / candidate / list registration** | The most analytically central stage for our project. Who can submit a list — registered party, voter grouping, citizen committee, independent. Signature thresholds, deposits, deadlines, equal-treatment rules between party and non-party lists. |
| 6 | **Media coverage** | Statutory rules on access to broadcast and public space, especially equal-time provisions and how they treat non-party lists. |
| 7 | **Campaign finance** | Public funding eligibility, donation and spending caps, disclosure. The party-vs-non-party-list asymmetry is often most visible here. |
| 8 | **Voting process** | What the voter actually does: ballot type (closed/open/flexible), preference votes, panachage, cumulation, polling-day mechanics. |
| 9 | **Vote count** | Counting authority, audit and recount rules, the inter-list seat allocation formula (D'Hondt, Sainte-Laguë, Hare/LR, Droop), threshold application, list-cartel/apparentement mechanics. |
| 10 | **Results** | Intra-list seat assignment, declaration of winners, vacancy rules, certification timelines. |
| 11 | **Electoral authorities** | The institutional design of the election-management body: centralized vs. decentralized, independence and tenure, dispute-resolution forums. |

## Why this spine

- **Statutes follow the cycle**: most electoral codes are organized in
  this rough sequence, so coding statutes against the cycle is an
  efficient extraction strategy.
- **Cross-comparable**: PEI uses the same 11 stages globally, which
  means our case studies feed naturally into a wider comparative
  conversation.
- **Diagnostic coverage**: the cycle catches stages our list-PR-specific
  dimensions framework underweights — particularly district boundaries
  (#3), voter registration (#4), and media access (#6).

## What the spine alone is not enough for

The cycle tells us **when** in the electoral process a rule operates,
but not the **substantive content** of the rule (that is the structural
dimensions framework, `dimensions.md`) and not whether the rule is
**well-designed for democratic outcomes** (that is the performance
framework, `performance.md`). All three axes are needed.
