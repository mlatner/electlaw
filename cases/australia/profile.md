# Australia — case overview

Australia's interest for this project is **not** its
vote-conversion rule (STV is out of scope as a conversion rule)
but the **administrative interface** by which Australian
jurisdictions let voters express either a single group
preference or candidate-level preferences. The above-the-line /
below-the-line architecture is the closest working comparator
to the user's preferred ballot design (one-vote MMP with
party-or-candidate options).

## Sub-profiles

- **`nsw_local_profile.md`** — *Local Government Act 1993* (NSW)
  and *Local Government (General) Regulation 2021* (NSW). NSW
  council elections in multi-seat wards / undivided councils
  use **proportional representation with above-the-line group
  voting**. The substantive Australian local case for this
  project.
- **`senate_reference.md`** *(planned)* — *Commonwealth
  Electoral Act 1918*, Senate provisions. Federal Senate
  elections (STV with above-the-line) and the **2016 reform**
  abolishing group voting tickets. Reference comparator only —
  national, not local.
- **`tasmania_reference.md`** *(planned, optional)* — Tasmanian
  House of Assembly elections under the Hare-Clark system with
  Robson rotation. STV but with strong constraints on the
  ballot interface that contrast with NSW's group-voting
  approach.

## Why Australia is in the project

- **Above-the-line group voting is the canonical implementation
  of the user's preferred ballot architecture**: one mark for a
  group OR detailed preferences for individual candidates, both
  on the same ballot.
- The **2016 federal Senate reform** is the most recent serious
  legislative debate in any common-law democracy on exactly the
  design space we're working in — what should an above-the-line
  vote actually mean? Australia's answer evolved twice (1984,
  2016).
- NSW local councils are the **largest scale** at which a
  list-shaped above-the-line interface is currently in
  operation in any common-law jurisdiction.

## Cross-cutting research questions

When the Australian sub-profiles are populated:

1. How does the NSW above-the-line group voting interface
   differ from the federal Senate's post-2016 system, and what
   does that tell us about whether to allow voters to express
   preferences across groups versus only within a group?
2. How are non-party groups (analogous to the German
   *Wählergruppe*) accommodated under NSW's group registration
   regime, and how does that compare with the Bavarian
   residual definition (GLKrWG Art. 24(1))?
3. What does the comparative invalid-vote rate tell us about
   ballot complexity under the above-the-line option versus
   pure preference systems?
4. **Equity and minority representation**: How has above-the-line
   voting affected representation for Indigenous Australians and
   migrant communities at the local level? (To be developed in
   the eventual comparisons document.)
