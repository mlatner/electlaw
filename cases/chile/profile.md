# Chile — Cámara de Diputados, Senado, and Concejos Municipales

## 0. Case metadata

- **Country**: Republic of Chile (República de Chile)
- **Sub-jurisdiction**: National (Cámara de Diputados; Senado) and
  local (*concejos municipales*, 345 communes / *municipalidades*).
- **Levels of government covered**: This profile covers the national
  legislature — both Cámara de Diputados and Senado, restructured
  under **Ley Nº 20.840 (2015)** — and the municipal concejales,
  governed by **Ley Nº 18.695**.
- **Electoral system family**: **Open-list PR** at all three levels.
  Jefferson/D'Hondt allocation. Single nominal vote that
  simultaneously contributes to inter-list and intra-list ordering.
  No statutory threshold above the structural threshold of
  exclusion (1/(M+1)). Pacto electoral (apparentement) permitted;
  intra-pacto allocation is a second D'Hondt step.
- **Governing statutes**:
  - **Constitución Política de la República** (1980, with reforms),
    Arts. 18, 47, 49, 118.
  - **Ley Nº 18.700, Orgánica Constitucional sobre Votaciones
    Populares y Escrutinios** (LOC VPE) — the comprehensive
    statute for parliamentary and presidential elections; the
    cycle, ballot, count, and dispute-resolution rules. Originally
    enacted 6 May 1988; amended numerous times, most consequentially
    by Ley Nº 20.840 (2015).
  - **Ley Nº 18.695, Orgánica Constitucional de Municipalidades**
    (LOC Municipalidades) — the municipal-level statute including
    council-size table, the proportional formula for concejales,
    and the conduct of municipal elections (in conjunction with
    LOC VPE for matters of common application).
  - **Ley Nº 18.603, Orgánica Constitucional de los Partidos
    Políticos** — party registration, internal democracy,
    dissolution, and the prerequisites for forming pactos
    electorales.
  - **Ley Nº 20.840, de 5 de mayo de 2015** — *"Sustituye el
    sistema electoral binominal por uno de carácter proporcional
    inclusivo y fortalece la representatividad del Congreso
    Nacional"* (replaces the binominal electoral system with one
    of inclusive proportional character and strengthens the
    representativeness of the National Congress).
  - **Ley Nº 21.298, de 23 de diciembre de 2020** — established
    paritarian rules for the 2021 Constitutional Convention
    election; gender-parity precedent that informs subsequent
    legislative-level debates.
  - **Ley Nº 18.556, Orgánica Constitucional sobre Sistema de
    Inscripciones Electorales y Servicio Electoral** — establishes
    *Servel* (Servicio Electoral de Chile) as the electoral
    management body and the electoral roll.
- **Implementing instruments**: **Servel** (Servicio Electoral)
  resolutions and instructions; the **Tribunal Calificador de
  Elecciones (TRICEL)** for parliamentary and presidential
  qualification and disputes; **Tribunales Electorales Regionales
  (TER)** for municipal disputes.
- **Last regular elections covered**:
  - **Parliamentary (Diputados + half of Senado)**: 21 November
    2021, with presidential runoff 19 December 2021. Next: 16
    November 2025.
  - **Municipal (alcaldes + concejales)**: 26–27 October 2024
    (concurrent with regional gubernatorial elections).
- **Analyst**: this profile, 2026-05-15.

### Why this case is in the project

Chile is the project's principal case for a **recent, complete
replacement of a sub-proportional formula with proportional
allocation**. The 2015 reform under Ley Nº 20.840 abolished the
binominal system (M = 2 in every district, with a within-district
threshold structure that systematically favored the second-place
coalition) and substituted variable-magnitude districts (M = 3–8
for Diputados; M = 2–5 for Senado) with D'Hondt allocation. The
reform is the project's clearest example of how a constitutional
democracy moves from one statutory family to another by ordinary
parliamentary action (constitutional anchor reaffirmed; the operative
rule is in the LOC VPE, an *Ley Orgánica Constitucional* requiring
4/7 supermajority to amend).

Three features make Chile useful beyond the substantive reform:

1. **Multilevel coverage with a layered statutory regime.** The LOC
   VPE governs common provisions; the LOC Municipalidades adds the
   council-size table and intra-municipal mechanics. A drafter
   reading the Chilean code reads two interlocking organic laws
   rather than a single unified code (as in Spain's LOREG). The
   Chilean architecture is the project's example of *coordinated
   organic statutes* rather than a unified code.

2. **Pactos electorales with explicit two-step D'Hondt allocation.**
   Chile permits multi-party pre-electoral coalitions (*pactos
   electorales*) on the ballot. The Servel-administered formula
   first applies D'Hondt across pactos and unaffiliated lists for
   inter-list allocation; then applies D'Hondt within each pacto
   to distribute the pacto's seats among its constituent parties.
   Intra-party seat assignment follows nominal-vote order.

3. **Independent candidates with two distinct paths.** Independents
   may run either (a) on a party or pacto list (with party
   nomination and patrocinante signatures) or (b) on an independent
   list with population-tiered signature requirements collected
   from registered voters. The two paths produce different
   structural prospects for non-party candidacies.

4. **Gender parity by transitional rule, with reform under
   discussion.** Ley Nº 20.840's Article 3 transitorio established
   a 40/60 candidate-quota requirement (neither sex less than 40%
   nor more than 60% of a list's candidacies) applicable to the
   2017, 2021, 2025, and 2029 parliamentary cycles. The 2024
   municipal cycle did not apply the parliamentary quota to
   concejales; municipal parity reform is in legislative
   discussion. Chile's 2021 Constitutional Convention election
   under Ley Nº 21.298 deployed paritarian outcome rules (50/50
   parity-corrected outcomes), an outlier mechanism not extended
   to standing legislative elections.

For minority-representation analysis, Chile presents three patterns:
(a) **indigenous representation** through the limited
constitutional-convention reserved-seats precedent (also not
extended to the standing Congreso); (b) **geographic / regional
representation** through variable-magnitude districts; and (c)
**non-party candidacies** through the independent-list path. The
Mapuche, Aymara, and other indigenous peoples are not separately
represented in the Cámara de Diputados or Senado.

## 1. Statutory architecture — the LOC framework

Chile's electoral law operates across **two principal organic
statutes** (each requiring a 4/7 absolute-majority quorum to
amend, per Constitution Art. 66) plus a third party-law organic
statute, with Servel instructions cascading on top:

| Statute | Subject | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Constitución Política** (1980, with reforms) | Constitutional anchor: Art. 18 (Servel and electoral system reservation to organic law); Art. 47 (Cámara de Diputados, 155 seats, distritos); Art. 49 (Senado, 50 seats, circunscripciones); Art. 118 (concejales) | Constitutional |
| **Ley Nº 18.700** (LOC VPE) | Comprehensive electoral statute: registration of candidacies, pactos, ballot design, voting, vote count, allocation formula (D'Hondt), TRICEL review | LOC (4/7) |
| **Ley Nº 18.695** (LOC Municipalidades) | Municipal regime: council-size table; municipal allocation rules; alcalde and concejal contests; remits common procedure to LOC VPE | LOC (4/7) |
| **Ley Nº 18.603** (LOC Partidos Políticos) | Party formation, internal democracy, dissolution, pacto-formation prerequisites | LOC (4/7) |
| **Ley Nº 18.556** (LOC Sistema de Inscripciones / Servel) | Servel as the electoral management body; automatic enrollment from civil registry | LOC (4/7) |
| **Ley Nº 20.840** (2015 reform) | Substituted the binominal system; redefined districts, magnitudes, formula; transitional gender-parity Art. 3 transitorio | Amending act |

**Servel instructions** layer onto the statutes. Servel issues
*resoluciones* and *instrucciones* for each cycle that operationalize
LOC VPE provisions on candidate registration, ballot form,
patrocinante-signature procedures, and the application of the
D'Hondt formula. Servel's *Acuerdo* No. 32/2017 (and successor
acuerdos for the 2021 and 2025 cycles) operationalize the
post-2015 proportional system.

**Statute-instruction-jurisprudence cascade**:

1. **Constitution Art. 18** reserves the electoral system to
   organic-constitutional law and creates Servel as a constitutional
   organ with independent administrative function.
2. **LOC VPE and LOC Municipalidades** set the operative rules.
   As *leyes orgánicas constitucionales*, both require absolute
   4/7 supermajority for amendment. This places a structural
   barrier against majoritarian erosion comparable to Spain's
   LOREG (*ley orgánica*, simple absolute-majority requirement).
3. **Servel resolutions and instructions** resolve operational
   ambiguity. Servel maintains a public archive of acuerdos and
   resoluciones at <https://www.servel.cl>.
4. **TRICEL** (Tribunal Calificador de Elecciones, established
   by Const. Arts. 95–97 and its own organic law) qualifies
   results and resolves parliamentary disputes. **TERs** (Tribunales
   Electorales Regionales) handle municipal-level disputes at
   first instance.
5. **Tribunal Constitucional** reviews LOC amendments for
   constitutional conformity before promulgation (Const. Art. 93
   No. 1, *control preventivo obligatorio* for organic laws).

For U.S. drafters, the Chilean architecture documents an
alternative to the Spanish unified-code approach: **two
coordinated organic statutes** governing distinct but cross-
referencing subject-matter (electoral procedure plus municipal
governance), each amendable only by supermajority. The drafting
trade-off is that operational rules common to both levels (e.g.,
patrocinante signature authentication, ballot uniformity) sit in
LOC VPE and are imported by reference into LOC Municipalidades;
this places a premium on explicit cross-references but allows
each statute to evolve at its own pace.

## 2. Component 1 — Seat Product (Assembly Size × District Magnitude)

### Cámara de Diputados — Constitution Art. 47 and LOC VPE

Per the Constitution (Art. 47, as reformed in 2015) and LOC VPE
(arts. on districts and magnitudes, restated by Ley Nº 20.840):

- **Total seats**: **155** (raised from 120 by Ley Nº 20.840).
- **Districts**: **28 distritos electorales**.
- **Magnitude**: variable, **M = 3 to 8**, set district by
  district in the statute.
- **Term**: 4 years; full Cámara renewed each cycle.

The 28-district map and magnitudes were fixed in Ley Nº 20.840 by
listing each district by number, naming its component communes,
and assigning the magnitude. The full schedule appears in LOC VPE
as amended (a statutory schedule that requires LOC-quorum amendment
to redraw).

Reported magnitudes (compiled from Ley Nº 20.840 schedule;
verbatim per-district list to be retrieved from BCN):

- **M = 3**: smallest-magnitude districts (e.g., Distrito 26 in
  Aysén; Distrito 28 in Magallanes).
- **M = 8**: largest-magnitude districts (the Santiago metropolitan
  districts).
- **Median M ≈ 5–6**.

**Structural feature**: the 2015 reform deliberately raised the
minimum magnitude from 2 (binominal) to 3, with most districts at
M = 5–7. This shifted the structural threshold of exclusion
(1/(M+1)) from a uniform 33.3% (M=2) to a range of 11.1% (M=8) to
25% (M=3).

### Senado — Constitution Art. 49 and LOC VPE

- **Total seats**: **50** (raised from 38 by Ley Nº 20.840).
- **Circunscripciones**: **15 circunscripciones senatoriales**
  corresponding to the 15 regions of Chile (as of the 2018 creation
  of the Ñuble region; the schedule may now list 16 with the Ñuble
  addition, **to be verified against current BCN consolidated
  text**).
- **Magnitude**: variable, **M = 2 to 5**, set in the statute.
- **Term**: 8 years; half the Senate renewed every 4 years on a
  rotating basis. The rotation is fixed in transitional provisions
  of Ley Nº 20.840.

**Structural feature**: M = 5 in the most populous regional
circunscripciones (Metropolitana, Valparaíso, Biobío) and M = 2 or
3 in less populous regions. The 8-year term with mid-term half-
renewal produces a representation lag relative to short-term
political shifts.

### Concejos Municipales — Constitution Art. 118 and LOC Municipalidades Art. 72

Per LOC Municipalidades Art. 72 (council-size schedule):

| Commune population | Concejales |
|---|---:|
| up to 70,000 electors | 6 |
| 70,001 – 150,000 electors | 8 |
| more than 150,000 electors | 10 |

Each commune constitutes a single electoral district for concejales;
the concejo includes the directly elected *alcalde* (mayor), but the
mayor is elected separately in a single-office contest (FPTP,
plurality) and is not within the concejo's M for proportional
purposes.

**Reported total**: 345 *municipalidades* (and one *administración
local* for Antártica Chilena), with concejo sizes 6, 8, or 10
depending on registered electors at the commune.

### Notes — minority-representation implications of the seat product

The 2015 reform's substantive effect on the seat product is
exactly the swing from M=2 to a variable M=3-8 distribution.
The structural threshold of exclusion fell from a uniform 33.3%
(binominal) to a range of 11.1%–25% (post-2015 Diputados) and
16.7%–33.3% (post-2015 Senado). For dispersed minority groups,
the post-reform structure increases the proportional opportunity
in metropolitan districts (M = 7–8) substantially while leaving
small-region districts (M = 3 Diputados, M = 2 Senado) close to
their pre-reform exclusionary effects.

**Indigenous representation**: Chile's national legislature does
**not** include reserved indigenous seats. The 2021 Constitutional
Convention, established by Ley Nº 21.298, reserved 17 seats for
indigenous peoples in a separate election; this mechanism has not
been incorporated into the standing parliamentary regime. Mapuche
population is concentrated in the Araucanía and Biobío regions
(particularly Distrito 22 and Distrito 21 in Diputados); the
post-2015 Diputados magnitudes (M = 5–6 in those districts) leave
indigenous candidacies dependent on party / pacto inclusion rather
than on reserved-seat allocation.

**Geographic minorities (region-concentrated)**: the variable-
magnitude structure is more permissive to regionally concentrated
small parties than the prior binominal system was. A party
concentrated in Aysén (M = 3) or Magallanes (M = 3) faces a 25%
structural threshold rather than the 33.3% binominal threshold.

**Municipal-level**: the 6 / 8 / 10 schedule produces structural
thresholds of 14.3%, 11.1%, and 9.1% respectively. Small-commune
councils (M = 6) maintain a higher structural floor than mid-
or large-commune councils.

For U.S. drafters: the Chilean Diputados reform documents a
*step-change* in the threshold of exclusion accomplished through
ordinary legislation (Ley Nº 20.840) under a constitutional
framework that reserves electoral-system design to organic law.
The substantive lesson is that **changing district magnitudes is
the most direct lever on the structural threshold of exclusion**,
more so than changing the formula (D'Hondt remained the implicit
allocation; what changed was M).

## 3. Component 2 — Ballot Structure

### Open list, single nominal vote (LOC VPE)

At all three levels covered by this profile, voters cast **a
single nominal vote for one candidate**. The ballot lists candidates
grouped by *lista* (list) — where a list is either a single party,
an electoral *pacto* (coalition of two or more parties), or an
*independent list* (pacto de independientes). The single nominal
vote serves two functions simultaneously:

1. **Inter-list allocation**: the vote contributes to its list's
   total, which determines how many seats the list wins under
   D'Hondt.
2. **Intra-list assignment**: the vote contributes to the
   candidate's personal total, which determines whether the
   candidate is among the top vote-getters within the list, in
   the order that fills the seats the list won.

Unlike Brazil's *voto de legenda* path, **Chile does not provide
for a list-only vote**. A voter who wishes to support a list
without expressing a candidate preference has no statutory
mechanism for doing so. The single mark must identify a specific
candidate; the vote then accrues both to the candidate and to the
list. Blank votes and null votes are recorded separately and do
not contribute to any list total.

### Ballot architecture

The ballot (*cédula electoral*) for parliamentary and concejal
contests displays:

- Candidate names grouped by list, with list labels (party
  initials, pacto names, or "Independientes" for independent
  lists).
- Each candidate is identified by name, with a marking position
  (a numbered box or oval) adjacent.
- Within a list, candidate order is determined by the submitting
  entity (party / pacto / independent list) and printed in that
  order; the order has informational salience for the voter but
  is not binding on seat assignment, which is determined by the
  candidates' personal vote totals (open-list logic).

### List-bearing entities (LOC VPE; LOC Partidos Políticos)

Lists may be presented by:

- **Partidos políticos** registered under Ley Nº 18.603 — alone or
  jointly with other parties as a pacto.
- **Pactos electorales** — formal pre-electoral coalitions of two
  or more registered parties, declared to Servel before the
  cycle. The pacto submits a single list; internal allocation of
  list positions among constituent parties is the pacto's
  internal matter (subject to LOC VPE and Servel registration
  rules).
- **Independent candidates** — through two distinct paths:
  - **On a party / pacto list**: a single independent candidate
    may be included on a registered party's or pacto's list,
    subject to that party's or pacto's nomination and the
    candidate's collection of patrocinantes (sponsor signatures).
  - **Independent list / pacto de independientes**: a group of
    independent candidates may form an *independent list*
    submitted jointly. This path requires patrocinante signatures
    per candidate and was procedurally restructured by reforms
    around the 2021 cycle to ease the formation of independent
    lists.

### Patrocinante signatures (independents and certain candidacies)

Independent parliamentary and concejal candidates must collect
**patrocinante signatures** — sponsor declarations from registered
voters in the district or commune. The number scales with the
electoral population. **Specific numbers and the population
scaling to be retrieved from current BCN consolidated text of
LOC VPE and LOC Municipalidades** (the figures have been amended
multiple times; the project's substantive-source rule requires
primary text rather than secondary summary).

A voter may sign the patrocinante declaration of **only one
candidate per contest** under standard Servel practice; this
parallels the LOREG Art. 187(2) / Bavarian Art. 28 GLKrWG single-
list-only rule.

### Gender parity — Ley Nº 20.840 Art. 3 transitorio

The 2015 reform established a **40/60 candidate-quota requirement**
in Article 3 transitorio of Ley Nº 20.840, applicable to the
2017, 2021, 2025, and 2029 parliamentary cycles. The provision
requires:

- Of the candidacies submitted by a party or pacto in each
  contest (Cámara de Diputados; Senado), **neither sex may
  represent less than 40% nor more than 60%** of the candidates.
- The rule applies to **candidacies** (list composition), not to
  electoral *outcomes*. Seat assignment proceeds by the open-list
  nominal-vote rule without sex-correction.
- Servel verifies compliance at the candidate-registration stage;
  non-compliant lists are rejected.

The rule is **transitional** by its text — the article specifies
the four cycles to which it applies. As of the date of this
profile, a permanent gender-parity provision has not been enacted
for legislative elections; reform discussion is ongoing.

The 2024 municipal cycle was held **without** application of the
parliamentary 40/60 quota to concejal lists. **A gender-parity
provision specific to municipal concejales is to be confirmed
against current LOC Municipalidades text**; reform proposals are
in legislative discussion.

The 2021 Constitutional Convention election operated under a
separate paritarian rule (Ley Nº 21.298) that combined candidate
parity with **parity-corrected outcomes** (post-count adjustment
to enforce 50/50 representation). That outcome-correction rule has
not been extended to standing legislative elections.

### Notes — minority-representation implications of ballot architecture

The open-list architecture places the *intra-list ranking decision*
in the voter's hands: nominal-vote totals determine which candidates
of a winning list take its seats. This is structurally distinct
from Spain's closed list (submitter-set order) and from Brazil's
voto de legenda mechanism (which gives voters an explicit "party
only" option).

For minority representation, the Chilean ballot architecture has
two consequential properties:

1. **List composition matters but is not decisive.** A minority
   candidate placed mid-list by a party is not penalized by list
   order (in contrast to Spain); the candidate competes with
   list-mates on the basis of personal vote totals. This shifts
   weight from party gatekeepers to voter personal-vote behavior.

2. **No list-only vote means minority-strategy coordination is
   constrained.** A voter sympathetic to a minority-aligned party
   cannot cast a vote for the party without choosing among its
   candidates; the absence of a *voto de legenda* path means each
   voter must select a specific candidate. For a minority
   candidate competing with list-mates from the same party, the
   personal-vote requirement creates intra-list competition that
   can fragment minority support.

The 40/60 gender quota (Ley Nº 20.840 Art. 3 transitorio) is
structurally **candidate-side** (list composition) rather than
outcome-side. It has produced measurable increases in women's
candidacies but smaller increases in seat shares, because the
open-list intra-list competition does not guarantee that women
candidates' personal votes track the list's total. The 2017 and
2021 elections produced women's seat shares in the Cámara of
**22.5%** (2017, post-reform first cycle) and **35.5%** (2021)
— increases against pre-reform baselines but below the 40/60
candidate-side quota itself.

For U.S. drafters: the Chilean candidate-side quota (Art. 3
transitorio) is a portable model for *statutory list composition
requirements* in open-list PR, with the documented caveat that
candidate-side parity does not mechanically translate to outcome
parity under open-list nominal voting. Closed-list quotas with
"zipper" alternation (Spain LOREG Art. 44 bis; many European
systems) produce tighter candidate-outcome coupling.

## 4. Component 3 — Allocation Formula

### Jefferson/D'Hondt with two-step pacto allocation

Chile applies the **Jefferson/D'Hondt method** (highest-averages
divisor method, with divisors 1, 2, 3, …) at all three levels
covered by this profile. The application differs between contests
with pactos and contests without pactos:

**Step 1 — Inter-list allocation across the district**:

D'Hondt is applied across all lists (parties, pactos, independent
lists) competing in the district. Each list's total = sum of all
nominal votes for its candidates. The divisor sequence 1, 2, 3, …
produces a ranked series of quotients; seats are awarded by
descending quotient order until the district magnitude is filled.

**Step 2 — Intra-pacto allocation (where pactos exist)**:

Within each pacto that won seats in Step 1, D'Hondt is applied a
second time using the constituent parties' subtotals. Each
constituent party's votes are divided by 1, 2, 3, …; the pacto's
seats are awarded to constituent parties by descending quotient
order until the pacto's Step-1 total is filled.

**Step 3 — Intra-party seat assignment (intra-list ordering)**:

Within each party (or independent list), seats are awarded to the
**top-N candidates by personal vote total**, where N is the
party's Step-2 allocation. Open-list logic: the candidates with
the most personal votes take the seats.

### Statutory locus

The allocation procedure is set in **LOC VPE Título IV**
(Escrutinios y Calificación de Elecciones) in the articles
restated by Ley Nº 20.840. **Verbatim Spanish text of the
controlling article (the post-2015 D'Hondt provision) and the
intra-pacto allocation article is to be retrieved from BCN
consolidated text**; this profile flags the gap rather than
back-fill from secondary summary.

The municipal allocation rule is set in **LOC Municipalidades**
(Título VI on the Junta Electoral / concejal election); the rule
parallels the parliamentary D'Hondt logic with adjustments for
the smaller council sizes (M = 6, 8, 10). **Verbatim Spanish
text of the controlling municipal article is also to be
retrieved**; secondary commentary describes the rule as
substantively equivalent to the parliamentary version.

### No statutory threshold above the structural floor

Chile does **not** impose a legal threshold (vote-share floor)
above the structural threshold of exclusion (1/(M+1)). A list
that wins enough votes to exceed the divisor-quotient for the
last seat in its district wins that seat regardless of national
vote share. This places Chile in the same family as Spain (where
the 3% Congress threshold is non-binding in most provinces) and
Brazil (which has a candidate-level 10% threshold but no party-
level minimum). The post-2015 Chilean system documents that **a
proportional system without a statutory threshold is
operationally viable in a large legislature** (155 Diputados).

### Pactos — apparentement in operation

Pactos are the project's principal Latin American example of
**permitted, contemporaneous apparentement** that operates through
a two-step D'Hondt allocation. Three features:

1. **Pacto formation is pre-electoral.** Parties wishing to form
   a pacto must declare it to Servel before candidate-registration
   close. Once declared, the pacto submits a single combined list
   in each district.
2. **Pacto votes are the sum of all member-party nominal votes
   plus any pacto-list independent's nominal votes.** The pacto
   competes in Step 1 against other pactos and unaffiliated
   parties.
3. **Step-2 intra-pacto distribution by D'Hondt on member-party
   subtotals**, with intra-party seat assignment by personal-vote
   order.

Brazil abolished proportional *coligações* in 2017 (EC 97/2017);
Chile has retained pactos under the 2015 reform. The contrast is
useful for U.S. drafting: the **two-step D'Hondt structure makes
pactos statutorily transparent** — the share of the pacto's seats
that each member party receives is determined by an explicit
divisor procedure on the member parties' own vote totals, not by
intra-coalition political negotiation.

### Notes — minority-representation implications of allocation

D'Hondt's structural large-list bias is present in Chile as in
Spain and the German Bundestag. In small-magnitude districts
(M = 3 Diputados; M = 2 Senado), D'Hondt strongly favors the top
one or two lists. In M = 7–8 Diputados districts, D'Hondt is
closer to proportional but still favors larger lists at the
margin.

For minority representation specifically:

- **Indigenous candidacies on party / pacto lists**: subject to
  Step-3 intra-list competition with party list-mates by personal
  votes. A candidate prominent within their list can win a seat
  the list takes; a candidate placed on the list but not
  competitive in personal votes does not.

- **Independent lists**: subject to the same Step-1 D'Hondt as
  party / pacto lists. A small independent list (a few
  candidates, no apparentement partners) competes against larger
  pactos and parties in a winner-take-(more)-than-proportional
  formula. The 2021 Cámara cycle returned a small number of
  independent-list candidates; the 2021 Constitutional Convention
  cycle (under different rules) returned a substantially larger
  number, but that is a one-off precedent.

- **Pacto effect**: the two-step structure means a small party
  joining a larger pacto can benefit from the pacto's overall
  Step-1 share without independently clearing the structural
  threshold — provided the small party clears the *intra-pacto*
  Step-2 D'Hondt. Pactos are accordingly the principal access
  path for small parties under the post-2015 system.

For U.S. drafters: the Chilean post-2015 system documents that
**proportional allocation in moderate-magnitude districts (M = 5–8)
with no statutory threshold and with permitted apparentement
produces seat allocations meaningfully more proportional than
either FPTP or low-magnitude PR**. The pacto mechanism is a
portable apparentement model: it permits small parties to
contest collectively while maintaining each member party's
identity and individual vote-share record (Step 2 makes the
intra-coalition allocation transparent).

## 5. Connected areas

### A. Voter eligibility and registration (Const. Art. 13; LOC Sistema de Inscripciones)

- **Eligibility**: Chilean citizens 18+ at the date of election.
  Foreign residents with 5+ years of legal residence may vote in
  all elections (Const. Art. 14) — a constitutionally entrenched
  franchise extension that places Chile among the small set of
  democracies with broad non-citizen voting rights at the national
  level.
- **Automatic enrollment** (since Ley Nº 20.568, 2012):
  registration is automatic for all eligible voters drawn from the
  civil registry (*Registro Civil*).
- **Compulsory voting**: re-established by **Ley Nº 21.524** for
  the 2024 municipal and subsequent cycles, after a 2012–2022
  interval of voluntary voting under Ley Nº 20.568. Failure to
  vote without justification triggers an administrative fine; the
  specifics are administered by Servel.

### B. Candidate / list registration — *full subsection*

This is the most important connected area for the project.

**Statutory locus**: LOC VPE Título II (Postulación de
Candidatos) and LOC Municipalidades parallel provisions.

**For partisan candidacies**:

- **Party affiliation**: candidates running on a party or pacto
  list must be affiliated with the sponsoring party (or be an
  individual independent included on the list under specified
  conditions). Ley Nº 18.603 governs the internal party
  procedures (primary election or party convention) by which
  parties select their candidates.
- **Primary elections** (LOC VPE arts. on primaries, as restated
  by Ley Nº 20.640 *Sobre Primarias para la Nominación de
  Candidatos*, 2012): parties may opt into a Servel-administered
  primary; results are binding on the party's nomination.

**For independent candidacies**:

- **Patrocinante signatures**: independents (on a party list or
  on an independent list) must collect patrocinante signatures
  from registered voters in the district / commune. **The exact
  numbers, the population-tiering, and the verification procedure
  are in LOC VPE Title II / LOC Municipalidades and require
  retrieval from BCN consolidated text**.
- **Verification**: signatures are authenticated through Servel
  procedures (notarial authentication and / or Servel direct
  verification). A voter may sign **for only one candidate per
  contest**.
- **Independent list (pacto de independientes)**: groups of
  independents may combine to form a list; each candidate
  collects signatures individually, and the combined list is
  presented to Servel. **Specific signature thresholds for
  independent lists — to be verified against current text**.

**Common requirements** (LOC VPE and LOC Munic.):

- **Filing deadline**: candidacy registration closes a specified
  number of days before the election (typically 120–150 days; to
  verify).
- **Disclosure obligations**: candidates disclose declarations of
  patrimony and interests under transparency rules administered
  by Servel.
- **Gender quota**: for parliamentary contests, the 40/60 Ley
  Nº 20.840 Art. 3 transitorio applies; Servel rejects non-
  compliant lists at registration. The provision does not apply
  to municipal concejal lists as of the date of this profile.

### C. Campaign finance (Ley Nº 19.884)

Campaign finance is governed by **Ley Nº 19.884, sobre
Transparencia, Límite y Control del Gasto Electoral** (2003,
substantially reformed by Ley Nº 20.900, 2016). Key features:

- **Spending limits** per candidate, scaled by district / commune
  electorate.
- **Public funding** (*reembolso del gasto electoral*): partial
  reimbursement of demonstrated campaign expenses for candidates
  exceeding a vote threshold.
- **Donation limits and disclosure**: caps on individual
  donations; ban on corporate donations to candidates (introduced
  by Ley Nº 20.900 in 2016 after the *Penta* and *SQM* campaign-
  finance scandals).
- **Servel administration and TER / TRICEL enforcement**: Servel
  audits candidate accounts; TER imposes sanctions for violations
  at municipal level; TRICEL at parliamentary level.

The 2016 reform package (Ley Nº 20.900 + companion statutes) is a
substantial Chilean precedent for tightening campaign-finance
disclosure and enforcement after corruption investigations
exposed evasion of pre-reform rules. The reform tied campaign-
finance compliance to candidate eligibility through Servel
verification of post-cycle accounts.

### D. Election administration — Servel

**Servicio Electoral de Chile (Servel)**:

- Constitutional anchor: Const. Art. 18 (autonomous body with
  legal personality and independent administrative function).
- Statutory anchor: Ley Nº 18.556 (LOC Sistema de Inscripciones
  Electorales y Servicio Electoral).
- **Consejo Directivo** of 5 members appointed by the President
  with Senate ratification (2/3 vote); 8-year non-renewable
  terms.
- **Director** appointed from candidates selected through Alta
  Dirección Pública (senior public-service appointment process).

**Servel responsibilities**:

- Maintain the electoral roll (Padrón Electoral).
- Register parties and pactos.
- Verify candidate registration including patrocinante signatures
  and gender-quota compliance.
- Publish ballot designs.
- Aggregate national vote results and submit to TRICEL for final
  qualification.
- Administer campaign-finance disclosure and reimbursement.
- Issue *resoluciones* and *instrucciones* operationalizing LOC
  VPE and LOC Municipalidades.

**Servel resolutions and instructions** layer onto the statutes
in a manner analogous to JEC instructions in Spain. Servel
*acuerdos* are numbered, dated, and publicly archived. They
constitute a body of administrative precedent that interprets
LOC VPE provisions on operational matters (signature
authentication, ballot form, list-composition verification).

For U.S. drafters: Servel's constitutional anchoring (Const. Art.
18) is a stronger institutional protection than the U.S. analog
(no constitutional EMB at the federal level; state-level EMBs of
varying independence). The Chilean model documents an EMB
designed with independence from executive control: 8-year non-
renewable Council terms staggered against the political cycle.

### E. Vote count, certification, dispute resolution

**Polling-level count**:

- Mesas receptoras de sufragios (polling-station boards) — staffed
  by citizen volunteers, with verification by Servel-appointed
  apoderados (party / pacto observers).
- Polling-station-level *acta de escrutinio* records the per-list
  and per-candidate counts.

**Aggregation and qualification**:

- Provincial / regional aggregation by Servel.
- **TER (Tribunales Electorales Regionales)** — 16 regional
  tribunals; first-instance qualification of municipal results
  and resolution of municipal-level disputes (LOC TER, separate
  organic law).
- **TRICEL (Tribunal Calificador de Elecciones)** —
  constitutional tribunal (Const. Arts. 95–97); final
  qualification of parliamentary and presidential results;
  appeals from TER on municipal matters.
- **Tribunal Constitucional** — *control preventivo obligatorio*
  of LOC amendments before promulgation (Const. Art. 93 No. 1);
  also resolves constitutional challenges to statutory provisions.

The cascade: polling-station mesas → Servel aggregation → TER
(municipal) or TRICEL (parliamentary) qualification → Tribunal
Constitucional for constitutional questions.

### F. Media access

- **Franja electoral (electoral broadcasting band)**: per LOC VPE,
  free broadcast time is allocated to parties, pactos, and
  independent lists for presidential and parliamentary elections.
  The allocation formula is set in LOC VPE; the duration and
  scheduling are administered by Servel in coordination with the
  Consejo Nacional de Televisión (CNTV).
- **Independent lists**: receive franja time on the same terms as
  party lists, prorated by the number of candidates and the
  list's geographic scope. The 2021 cycle was the first in which
  independent-list franja allocations operated under restructured
  rules favoring independent-list visibility.
- **Concejal elections**: more limited franja allocation; the
  scale and budget are smaller for municipal cycles.

### G. (Districting law — captured under Component 1)

District structure for Diputados (28 distritos, M = 3–8) and
Senado (15 or 16 circunscripciones, M = 2–5) is fixed in the
LOC VPE schedule set by Ley Nº 20.840. Redistricting requires
LOC amendment (4/7 of each chamber). For concejales, district =
commune; the council-size table in LOC Municipalidades Art. 72
is the only municipal-level "districting" parameter.

The 2015 reform redrew the parliamentary district map *as part
of the reform package*. This is distinct from a periodic
re-districting cycle (none exists); districting is by ordinary
LOC amendment.

## 6. PROSeS performance diagnostic — minority-representation focus

**Empirical anchors**: the **21 November 2021** parliamentary
election (second cycle under the post-2015 proportional system)
and the **26–27 October 2024** municipal elections.

**Sources**:

- *Servicio Electoral de Chile (Servel)* — official results at
  <https://www.servel.cl>.
- *Tribunal Calificador de Elecciones (TRICEL)* — final
  qualified results.
- Academic literature on the Chilean 2015 reform (notably the
  *Revista de Ciencia Política* (Pontificia Universidad Católica)
  symposium on the post-binominal system).

### Process Design

**Servel** is constitutionally anchored (Const. Art. 18) with
independent governance. The 2016 reform package (Ley Nº 20.900)
strengthened Servel's enforcement role over campaign finance.
**Tribunales electorales** (TER + TRICEL) operate as a
specialized adjudicative branch outside the ordinary judiciary;
this parallels Brazil's Justiça Eleitoral but with thinner
staffing (TERs are part-time tribunals).

### Resource Investment

Servel's annual budget is set in the national budget law
(Ley de Presupuestos). The 2024 cycle deployed Servel's first
fully integrated digital aggregation system (introduced in
2021 cycle); paper ballots remain primary for ballot casting.

### Service Output Quality

**Convenience**: in-person voting only (no postal voting; no
early voting). Voters vote at assigned polling stations within
their commune of registration.

**Accuracy**: paper ballots, hand-counted at the polling station
by mesa receptora staff, aggregated by Servel. The system
maintains a paper trail and is publicly auditable. Invalid-vote
rates at the 2021 parliamentary election were ~2.1% (voto nulo
+ blanco); at the 2024 municipal cycle ~4.5% (concejal
contests).

**Enforcement**: compulsory voting was re-established by Ley
Nº 21.524 for 2024 and subsequent cycles. Failure to vote
without justification triggers an administrative fine (~0.5–3
UTM under current rules). Enforcement at the 2024 cycle was
operational but uneven; reform discussion is ongoing.

### Service Outcomes — minority representation

**Indigenous representation (Mapuche and others)**:

The Cámara de Diputados does **not** include reserved indigenous
seats. Indigenous candidates run on party / pacto lists. The 2021
Cámara cycle returned a small number of self-identified Mapuche
deputies (the exact count varies by self-identification
criteria; commonly cited figures are 2–4 of 155, against an
indigenous population estimated at 12–13% of the Chilean
population).

The **2021 Constitutional Convention** election under Ley
Nº 21.298 reserved **17 of 155 seats** for indigenous peoples
through a separate electoral mechanism. The Convention completed
its draft in 2022; the proposed constitution was rejected in the
4 September 2022 plebiscite. A second constitutional process in
2023 (Consejo Constitucional under Ley Nº 21.533) did not extend
the reserved-seats mechanism; that process's proposed text was
also rejected in the 17 December 2023 plebiscite. The reserved-
seats precedent thus remains a *one-cycle precedent at the
constitutional-convention level*, not part of the standing
parliamentary regime.

For U.S. drafters interested in reserved-seat mechanisms, the
Chilean 2021 Convention experience is a documented working
implementation. The political fact that the broader
constitutional reform was rejected does not directly bear on
the technical workability of the reserved-seats mechanism, which
operated administratively without incident.

**Geographic / regional minority representation**:

The post-2015 variable-magnitude structure produces meaningful
representation for regionally-concentrated small parties. The
2021 cycle returned representatives from regional parties /
movements in Aysén, Magallanes, and Tarapacá that would have
been excluded under the binominal system.

**Gender representation**:

- **Cámara de Diputados**: women's seat share rose from ~15.8%
  (2013, pre-reform last binominal cycle) to **22.5%** (2017,
  first post-reform cycle) and **35.5%** (2021). The 40/60
  candidate quota (Ley Nº 20.840 Art. 3 transitorio) is
  candidate-side; outcome shares trail the candidate-side
  quota.
- **Senado**: smaller seat-share gains given the longer cycle and
  smaller magnitudes; women held approximately 12.0% (2017)
  rising to 23.5% (2021).
- **Municipal concejos**: the 2024 cycle was held without
  application of the parliamentary 40/60 quota to concejal
  lists; women's share of concejal seats was approximately
  **27–28%** (to verify against final TRICEL / Servel data).

For U.S. drafters: the Chilean post-2015 experience documents
the candidate-side / outcome-side gap in open-list PR with
candidate quotas. The drafting lesson is that under open-list
nominal voting, **candidate-side quotas alone do not produce
outcome parity**; a "zipper" alternation requirement (Spain
LOREG Art. 44 bis sections-of-5; Argentine national chamber
list-position parity rule) tightens the linkage but requires
closed or "flexible" lists to operate, not pure open lists.

### Stakeholder Satisfaction

Public-opinion data on the post-2015 system (Centro de Estudios
Públicos surveys; LAPOP barometer) show mixed evaluations:

- The replacement of the binominal system is broadly supported as
  a fairness gain.
- Concerns about parliamentary fragmentation (the post-2015
  Cámara has typically returned 10–12 lists with seats) are a
  recurrent reform-discussion item; proposals for a 2.5–5%
  legal threshold have been raised but not enacted.
- Compulsory-voting reinstatement (Ley Nº 21.524, 2023) was
  controversial in legislative debate; satisfaction with the
  re-imposition has been positive in subsequent surveys, given
  the marked turnout increase at the 2024 cycle.

## 8. Gardner portability assessment for US implementation

| Provision | Type | Rationale |

| Independent-list path (pacto de independientes) with population-tiered patrocinantes | **Universal** | A direct model for U.S. drafting of non-party-list ballot access. The two paths (individual independent on a party list; independent list as a group) are both portable. |
| Servel constitutional anchoring (Const. Art. 18) | **Hybrid** | Substantively portable as a model for EMB independence; constitutional anchoring requires a state constitutional / charter amendment in U.S. context. |
| LOC quorum (4/7 of each chamber) for electoral-law amendment | **Hybrid** | The substantive idea — a supermajority requirement for electoral-law change — is portable; the specific 4/7 ratio is a Chilean choice. |
| 40/60 candidate gender quota with Servel list-rejection enforcement (Ley 20.840 Art. 3 transitorio) | **Hybrid** | The technical drafting is portable; U.S. constitutional posture on sex-based candidacy quotas is more constrained. The Servel-rejects-non-compliant-lists enforcement model is itself portable as an EMB-enforcement design. |
| Reserved indigenous seats (2021 Constitutional Convention precedent) | **Particular** | A one-cycle constitutional-convention mechanism; not extended to standing parliamentary regime. The technical design (separate ballot, separate vote pool, fixed seat reserve) is documented but the political durability of the design within Chile is unresolved. |
| Compulsory voting (Ley 21.524) | **Particular** | Politically and constitutionally untenable in U.S. contexts. |
| Foreign-resident voting (Const. Art. 14, 5-year residence) | **Particular** | Reflects Chilean constitutional choice; some U.S. local non-partisan contexts have parallel provisions but the constitutional posture differs. |

### Top three takeaways for US state/local implementation

1. **Variable-magnitude districts under a unified formula are a
   direct lever on the threshold of exclusion.** The Chilean
   2015 reform substituted M = 3–8 (Diputados) for the uniform
   M = 2 binominal system, dropping the structural threshold of
   exclusion from a uniform 33.3% to a range of 11.1%–25%. The
   formula did not change (D'Hondt); what changed was M. For U.S.
   state-level PR adoption, the **district-magnitude schedule is
   the most consequential drafting parameter** for the
   proportionality of the resulting system; formula choice
   (D'Hondt vs Sainte-Laguë vs Hare) produces smaller marginal
   effects than M choice does.

2. **Two-step D'Hondt allocation for pactos makes apparentement
   procedurally transparent.** Under the Chilean system, a
   pacto's overall share is determined by inter-list D'Hondt
   (Step 1); the pacto's allocation among its constituent parties
   is determined by intra-pacto D'Hondt on member-party vote
   subtotals (Step 2). Member-party seat shares are accordingly
   computed from member-party vote totals, not from intra-
   coalition negotiation. For U.S. drafting that contemplates
   permitted apparentement (analogous to Danish *listeforbund* or
   Finnish *vaaliliitto*), the Chilean two-step formula is a
   procedurally explicit model that avoids the opacity of single-
   step apparentement.

3. **Candidate-side gender quotas in open-list PR do not produce
   outcome parity without additional mechanism.** Chile's 40/60
   candidate quota (Ley Nº 20.840 Art. 3 transitorio) raised
   women's candidacy share to the 40% floor but produced Cámara
   outcome shares of 22.5% (2017) and 35.5% (2021) — measurable
   gains but below the candidate-side floor. The drafting lesson
   for U.S. adopters is that **open-list nominal voting weakens
   the candidate-outcome linkage that closed-list "zipper"
   quotas (Spain LOREG Art. 44 bis) produce**. A U.S. statute
   aiming at outcome parity through list-composition rules
   should consider whether the underlying ballot architecture
   supports candidate-outcome coupling.
