# Chile — local elections

*Concejales* (municipal councilors) elected under the *Ley Orgánica
Constitucional de Municipalidades* and the general electoral law,
using D'Hondt with open lists. Independents may run within party lists
(*pacto*) under specific conditions, and independent-only lists are
also permitted.

The Chilean experience is especially relevant after the 2015 reform
that ended the binominal system and the broader debates about how
independents access proportional ballots — sharpened further by the
2021 Constitutional Convention experience with reserved independent
candidacies.

## Open questions to resolve

- Current rules (post-2015) for non-party / independent lists at the
  municipal level: signature requirements, deposits, deadlines.
- Independents inside party pacts vs. independents on independent
  lists — different statutory paths and different practical outcomes.
- D'Hondt application: at the *pacto* level first, then within?
- Gender parity rules at the municipal level and how they bind
  independent lists.
- Lessons from the Constitutional Convention's independent-list rules
  (even though those are not municipal).
