# New Zealand — national MMP

National parliamentary elections under the *Electoral Act 1993* and
the *Electoral Referendum Act 1993*. Mixed-Member Proportional (MMP)
since 1996, adopted by referendum after a Royal Commission report.
A canonical working example of MMP in our case set, and the
strongest comparator for any US state-legislature-level reform that
might adopt MMP.

## Why this case is in the project

- **Canonical MMP**: 120 seats; ~72 single-member electorates plus
  ~48 list seats allocated nationwide using **Sainte-Laguë (Webster)**
  for compensatory leveling.
- **Threshold mechanics**: 5% party-vote threshold, *or* one
  electorate seat — a textbook example of "threshold with a back
  door" that has shaped New Zealand politics.
- **Reserved Māori seats**: minority representation handled through a
  separate roll mechanism, an instructive design feature for US
  contexts that need to address racial-minority representation under
  any reform.
- **Adoption process**: the 1992–93 referendum sequence is itself a
  model for democratic adoption of PR in a former plurality regime,
  paralleling the US ballot-measure path.

## Open questions to resolve

- Statutory text of the Sainte-Laguë allocation: how is it expressed
  in the *Electoral Act 1993*? By name, by formula, by procedural
  description, or by worked example?
- Statutory text of the threshold rule, including the "one
  electorate seat" backdoor — exact citation and any case law.
- Reserved Māori seat mechanics: how the dual roll interacts with
  the list-tier compensation.
- Overhang seats and how the statute treats them.
- Local government in NZ: which councils use STV (since 2004), which
  use FPTP — sub-case for *administrative scaffolding* under
  Component 2 even though STV is out of scope as a conversion rule.
- Funding regime for parties and lists, especially treatment of
  small / minor parties at and below the threshold.
