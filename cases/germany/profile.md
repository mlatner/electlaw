# Germany — case overview

Germany is the most extensively developed single case in the project.
Each of the 16 *Länder* has its own *Kommunalwahlgesetz* governing
local elections,
and the federal *Bundeswahlgesetz* governs national parliamentary
elections under MMP. Because the federal regime and the *Länder*
regimes differ substantially, this case is split into multiple
sub-profiles.

## Sub-profiles

- **`federal_profile.md`** — Bundestag MMP under the
  *Bundeswahlgesetz* (BWahlG), as reformed in 2023 and modified by
  BVerfG (30 July 2024). The canonical comparator for any US
  state-legislature-level MMP reform.
- **`bavaria_profile.md`** ✅ — *Gemeinde- und
  Landkreiswahlgesetz* (GLKrWG, 7 Nov 2006, last amended 24 July
  2023) and *Bayerische Gemeindeordnung* Art. 31. Strong panachage
  and cumulation (up to 3 votes) tradition; *Wählergruppen* (voter
  groupings) defined residually under Art. 24(1); Sainte-Laguë
  allocation since 2018 (Art. 35); pure-preference intra-list
  voting (Art. 36). The project's central case for non-partisan
  list administration in a partisan-permissive local environment.
- **`baden-wuerttemberg_profile.md`** *(planned)* —
  *Kommunalwahlgesetz*. Cumulation; *unechte Teilortswahl*;
  voter groupings.
- **`hesse_profile.md`** *(planned)* — *Kommunalwahlgesetz* with
  panachage/cumulation since 2001 reform.
- **`nrw_profile.md`** ✅ — *Kommunalwahlgesetz* (KWahlG NRW,
  consolidated 28 June 2025). Closed-list **one-vote MMP-style**
  contrast to Bavaria within the same federal frame: single vote
  elects a Wahlbezirk candidate and counts for the closed reserve
  list, half-and-half tier integration via § 31, Sainte-Laguë
  allocation (restored after VerfGH NRW 20 May 2025), no
  statutory threshold at council level (after VerfGH NRW 21
  November 2017). The most extensively developed case in the project for
  constitutional jurisprudence on PR mechanics.

## Why Germany covers the most variation across the design space

- A working example of **MMP at the national level**
  — Components 1, 2, and 3 are designed together with deliberate
  attention to compensatory mechanics.
- The **most permissive intra-list voter choice** anywhere in
  continental Europe, at the local level (cumulation + panachage
  in BY/BW/HE/RP).
- *Wählergruppen* / *Wählervereinigungen* sit alongside parties at
  the local level and have been litigated extensively in the
  Federal Constitutional Court — so the legal status of a
  non-party list is well-developed.
- The *Parteiengesetz* draws an explicit boundary between parties
  and voter groupings, giving us a working model of the partisan /
  non-partisan distinction in legislation.

## Cross-cutting research questions

When all priority sub-profiles are populated:

1. How does the law differentiate between *Partei* and
   *Wählergruppe*, and what consequences flow from the distinction
   in funding, ballot access, broadcast access, and disclosure?
2. How is cumulation / panachage administered — what counts as a
   valid ballot, what error rates obtain, and how is the count
   organized?
3. What does the comparative experience across *Länder* tell us
   about the trade-off between voter choice (open list with
   cumulation) and ballot simplicity (closed list)?
