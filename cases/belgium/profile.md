# Belgium — case overview

Belgium is the project's principal case for **regional variation
within a single country** and for the **Imperiali allocation
formula** at the municipal level. Three structural features
make Belgium analytically distinct in the project's set:

1. **Three regional electoral regimes** for municipal elections
   — *Lokaal en Provinciaal Kiesdecreet* (Flanders), *Code de la
   démocratie locale et de la décentralisation* (Wallonia), and
   the Brussels-Capital municipal regime — each with its own
   substantive rules. Plus the German-speaking Community for
   the nine eastern municipalities.
2. **Imperiali formula** at the municipal level — the only
   working Imperiali jurisdiction in the project's set. Imperiali
   produces stronger large-party bias than D'Hondt and
   significantly stronger bias than Sainte-Laguë / Webster.
3. **Compulsory voting** at all levels through 2024; Flanders
   abolished compulsory voting at the municipal level for the
   2024 cycle (the first cycle under the post-2024 *Lokaal en
   Provinciaal Kiesdecreet* amendments).

## Sub-profiles

- **`federal_profile.md`** — Chamber of Representatives elections
  under the federal *Code Électoral* (Loi du 12 avril 1894).
  150 seats, 11 districts, D'Hondt, 5% per-district threshold.
- **`flanders_local_profile.md`** — Flemish municipal elections
  under the *Lokaal en Provinciaal Kiesdecreet* (8 July 2011, as
  amended through 2023). Imperiali allocation; 2024 reform of
  the *lijststem* effect on intra-list ordering.
- **`wallonia_local_profile.md`** *(planned)* — Walloon municipal
  elections under *Code de la démocratie locale et de la
  décentralisation*, Livre I, Titre IV.
- **`brussels_local_profile.md`** *(planned)* — Brussels-Capital
  Region municipal elections.

## Why Belgium is in the project

- The Imperiali formula at municipal level is the most
  large-party-favoring widely-used allocation rule in Europe.
  Comparing Imperiali against the German Sainte-Laguë and
  Brazilian D'Hondt-style hybrid extends the project's
  documentation of the formula-bias question.
- The three regional regimes within one country illustrate that
  national electoral architecture and local electoral
  architecture can be (and in Belgium are) substantially
  different — relevant for U.S. drafters considering local PR
  reform that would proceed independently of state or federal
  rules.
- The 2024 Flemish *lijststem* reform — abolishing the effect
  of list votes on intra-list candidate ordering while
  preserving the list vote for inter-list allocation — is a
  recent drafting precedent on the candidate-or-party
  voting-mode question that the project is tracking for U.S.
  implementation.
- The federal D'Hondt + 5% per-district threshold combination
  is a working comparator for U.S. drafters considering
  state-legislature-level PR.

## Cross-cutting research questions

When the priority sub-profiles are populated:

1. How does Imperiali compare to D'Hondt and Sainte-Laguë on
   working data from comparable district magnitudes?
2. What did the 2024 Flemish reform actually change in voter
   behavior and seat outcomes? (Awaiting post-cycle analysis.)
3. How does compulsory-voting abolition (Flanders, 2024)
   interact with proportional outcomes — did turnout drop
   produce changes in inter-list shares?
