## Description scaffold — structure only, owner writes the prose

Five moves, roughly 70 words each to land under 350:

1. **The gap.** US reform debate argues over *whether* to adopt PR; almost
   nothing addresses the statutory and administrative text that would enact it.
2. **The frame.** The three components — Seat Product (assembly size ×
   magnitude; threshold of exclusion 1/(M+1)), ballot structure, allocation
   formula — and the three analytical priorities this panel applies:
   legibility of the law, interconnection across the statutory cascade, and
   capacity for minority representation.
3. **The comparative base.** Twelve jurisdictions profiled on primary statutory
   text — Germany (federal, Bavaria, NRW), Australia NSW local, Brazil, Spain,
   Netherlands, Belgium, France, Chile, South Africa, New Zealand, Scotland,
   Denmark.
4. **The US problem.** US law gives independents a label, never a list;
   Connecticut is the only state carrying all three list components; the
   multi-seat case and the ballot-line case never coincide
   (`docs/us_law_of_lists.md`, 2026-10-06).
5. **Tie to the congress theme.** "Uncertain Democracies" — the drafting
   question is governance capacity, not preference aggregation.
